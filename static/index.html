The planispheric
astrolabe.

The rete carries star pointers and the ecliptic. Beneath it, the tympan represents the local sky for a chosen latitude. Rotating the rete models the daily turning of the celestial sphere against the local horizon.

MATER: the body and graduated limb that holds the instrument.

TYMPAN: the latitude-specific projection of the local sky.

RETE: the rotating star map and ecliptic.

RULE: an index for reading alignments and scales.

Solar position is calculated from date, local time, latitude and longitude using NOAA's approximate solar-position equations. The drawn rete is an educational stereographic model, not a calibrated historical replica.

The armillary
sphere.

The rings represent great circles of the celestial sphere. Set the observer's latitude and the date: the instrument changes its orientation and shows how the celestial equator, ecliptic and local horizon relate.

EQUATOR: celestial equator, projected from Earth's equator.

ECLIPTIC: apparent annual path of the Sun.

TROPICS: declination limits of the Sun, approximately ±23.44°.

HORIZON: local boundary between visible and hidden sky.

The polar
planimeter.

The pole arm is anchored. The tracer arm follows the boundary. A measuring wheel records the component of motion that accumulates into enclosed area. The wheel's total revolutions are proportional to the area traced.

ENCLOSED AREA0.00 cm²

The boundary integral is evaluated from the traced polygon; the displayed wheel revolutions are derived from the Amsler relation A = 2πLrN. This is a calibrated digital demonstration of the instrument's integrating principle, not a physical wheel simulation.

Trace a closed boundary to obtain a measurement. The number is useful only when the measured boundary has a clear definition.
